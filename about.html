About this tour

Teatro Antico di Taormina - simulated acoustics. A virtual acoustics tour of two computer models of the theatre: A, an intact Roman hypothesis, and B, a stylised model of the theatre in its present ruined state.

Simulated, not recorded

Simulated, not recorded. These responses are the predictions of an uncalibrated computational model (COMSOL Multiphysics® v. 6.4 ray acoustics; statistical phase synthesised from ray energy) of two states of the theatre: A, an intact Roman hypothesis, and B, the present ruin. This model has not been calibrated against measured impulse responses; it is what our model predicts, not what it sounded like.

  • The first-order ambisonic encoding is derived from ray arrival directions at a 1 m-radius collection cylinder around each seat.
  • On headphones you hear a simple left/right stereo decode of that first-order sound field (the template's default; no HRTF, so it is not binaural). It shows left-right direction as you turn, but not front-back or height: a sound ahead and a sound behind sound alike.
  • Reverberation-time metrics are unreliable outdoors.
  • No absolute level or speech-intelligibility claim is made. One common playback gain is used for every response in the tour, so level differences between seats, between A and B and between the empty and the full audience are kept, but the playback level is not the level in the theatre. The tour corrects for your device's audio sample rate, so the level does not depend on it.
  • Each response is one random realisation of the model's ray energy. A, B and the two audiences use independent realisations, so a small audible difference between them may be the random realisation rather than the acoustics.
  • Ray-count precision: direct sound is deterministic; reflected energy is sampled by a finite deterministic release lattice (400 000 rays per run). The study judges A/B and occupancy contrasts with matched-ray resampling, not by listening to two examples. Where few rays reached a seat, the sense of direction rests on few sampled arrivals.
  • The six octave bands (125 Hz to 4 kHz) are combined at the campaign's per-band basis powers, not at a talker's spectrum, so the tonal balance is not a measured voice spectrum.
  • The pictures are renders of the study website's drawn model of the theatre (https://yishanshen.com/acoustic/taormina/), made by the website's own rendering with one capture-only change to its shadow test (a slope-scaled depth bias that removes shadow-acne streaks; it changes a whole panorama by a mean of at most 0.53 of 255 code values), and seen from the website's own seat for each listening position, at seated eye height (1.2 m above the drawing's surface below). The website has no voice for eight seats per building (2I12, 3I03, 4I08, 5I12, 6I08, 7I03, 1M05 and 9M05) and does not seat a visitor there; for these the page was seated at the nearest seat that has one, with the camera at the seat's own eye (in a test this changed at most 1 pixel of 9 000 000). 1M05 the website keeps as a record only, with no drawn seat: its camera stands at the simulated listening position's place on the plan, 1.2 m above the drawing's surface, while the simulated listening position itself is 0.87 m (A) and 1.87 m (B) above that surface. The sound is simulated in the study's acoustic model, not in this drawing: the drawing follows published surveys and reconstructions and is not the geometry in which the sound was simulated; among other differences, its stage front stands 9.65 m (A) and 9.8 m (B) nearer the orchestra centre (4.675 m from it, against 14.325 m in A and 14.475 m in B in the acoustic model). Seen from the picture's position and heard at the simulated listening position, the direction toward the simulated voice position (in A's drawing it lies behind the front of the drawn stage building: y 19.22 m against 13.4 to 15.1 m) differs by at most 2.5 degrees to the side and 3.3 degrees in height. The pictures are not photographs and not a claimed historical appearance. In the acoustic model, B's ruined stage wall, including the size of its central gap, is a modelling assumption.
  • The acoustic model's terrain is a 2 m digital terrain model (DTM) crop (SITR/ICE); the pictures draw no terrain surface (see Credits).

The study's own statement of the model's limits:

"This model has not been calibrated against measured impulse responses from the Teatro Antico di Taormina; no public per-position dataset exists. Materials are literature values for comparable Greco-Roman theatres, cited individually. Ray acoustics does not model edge diffraction; low-frequency results near the scaenae-frons gap and seat rows carry extra uncertainty. Results are a physically consistent reconstruction with stated sensitivity ranges — what our model predicts, not what it sounded like."

Two words

Statistical: the model predicts how much sound energy reaches a seat at each moment, from which direction and in each octave band, and the fine detail of the waveform is then filled in with random noise shaped to that energy.

Uncalibrated: this project has no measured impulse responses of this theatre to compare with (no public per-position dataset exists), so the model has not been adjusted to or checked against measurements.

What the sound menu means

At each seat the menu "Sound source" chooses which simulated response the sample is played through. Positions are in the model's own coordinates (metres, from the orchestra centre on the orchestra floor), as resolved by the project's source definitions and recorded in each response's provenance file (source_position_m).

Voice on the stage - empty seats
A talker's mouth on the stage, 1.6 m above the stage deck, facing the audience (model position (0, 19.22, 3.9) m), with the directivity of a male speaking voice at normal effort (measured, sentence-averaged, adapted into band-averaged patterns). The seats are empty.
Voice on the stage - full audience
The same voice source, with the seating modelled as occupied by an audience.

The study also simulated an all-round (omnidirectional) reference source at the stage centre and at the orchestra centre, and a raised-voice variant of the voice. They are not offered here. The reference source has a fixed power of 10 mW, about 27 dB above the voice source's reference power (project source definitions), and at the same seat it played 24 to 35 dB louder than the voice. Under one common playback gain the voice would then be close to silence, and switching between them would jump by tens of decibels. Within this tour, levels can be compared between seats, between A and B, and between the empty and the full audience.

What you hear played

  • Soprano - O mio babbino caro (Puccini): an anechoic (echo-free) recording of a soprano singing "O mio babbino caro" from Puccini's Gianni Schicchi, from Dario D'Orazio's Anechoic recordings of Italian opera (University of Bologna). An excerpt (10.942 s) of the left channel is used; it loops. The singer's name is not supplied by the source and the microphone position is not documented by the source. It is a real recording made outside the theatre; everything you hear of the theatre comes from the simulated response. The model's voice source radiates like a male speaking voice; the soprano recording is played through it as a dry signal, so the result does not simulate a singer's own directivity.
  • Bacchae - Ancient Greek reading: the first 24 seconds of a reading of lines 1-63 of Euripides' Bacchae, narrated in Ancient Attic Greek by Ioannis Stratakis (Podium-Arts, YouTube). It is a studio reading with its own room acoustics, not an anechoic recording; it is played through the simulated response as it is, so the recording room is heard as well. It makes no claim about how an ancient performance sounded. It plays at a fixed playback gain of -9.44 dB, with Ambisonic Mode on and off, so that its loudest predicted peak through any of the responses stays at the soprano's.
  • Gladiator fight - sound effect: a produced sound-effect mix ("Gladiator Fight Sound Effect", Sound Effect Database, YouTube). It carries its own ambience and is not an anechoic recording; it is played through the simulated response as it is, as one emitter at the stage source (the voice source's position and directivity), so the separate sources of a fight are not placed in the theatre. It is a modern production, not a historical recording or reconstruction. It plays at a fixed playback gain of -2.13 dB, with Ambisonic Mode on and off, so that its loudest predicted peak through any of the responses stays at the soprano's.

With Ambisonic Mode (Wet) switched off you hear the dry recording alone, at one fixed playback level (+0.3 dB) chosen so that it sits near the median level of the simulated sound; the picture turns grey to show the change.

Mic plays your own microphone through the same responses: your voice is placed at the sound source on the stage, not at your seat, and the response includes the direct sound from the stage to the seat. It is not a reconstruction of how your own voice would sound from your seat. Use headphones, not loudspeakers, to avoid feedback. Upload plays a file you choose through the same responses. Both are processed in your browser; nothing is uploaded or recorded.

Seats and map

Each building has 24 listening seats. The dots on the map are these modelled seats, not every seat of the theatre. The same seat name is used in A and B; its position follows each model's seating, so it is not at exactly the same point in both. Left and right mean as seen by someone facing the stage; the map has the stage at the top and an arrow to true north (from the project's derived georeference, not a survey). In B the stage wall is drawn with its central gap (the model's assumed 13.97 m collapsed section).

Tour template

This tour is built with the CAVERN Template by Luna Leila Valentin and Celeste Betancur Gutierrez (github.com/lunavalentin/CAVERN_Template, commit f54c6afd), used under its non-commercial licence, which asks for that credit with a link to the repository when the template or a work built on it is published, presented or shared. Fixes to the template made for this tour were contributed to it and merged into it on 2026-10-29 by Luna Leila Valentin, one of its authors (pull request #1). This tour changes five of its files with fourteen small patches (A to N):

  • scripts/map.js (A): the map reads its bounds from the tour's settings instead of fixed values; seat dots and their touch areas are larger, and a seat is chosen when the click or tap is released on it.
  • scripts/main.js (B, F, I, M): the seat and the chosen sound source can be given in the web address (?loc=, ?src=), and the switch between A and B keeps both; the sound field turns with the picture in the measured direction (F), also while a reverberation rings out after Stop (M); the truth line is filled in; a seat chosen during a seat change is not dropped; only neighbouring panoramas are preloaded; the arrow keys turn the view; a seat is shown and sounds only once its own panorama is on screen: if its picture cannot be loaded, nothing is shown or played and a message below Start/Stop says so until the picture is shown, another seat is chosen (at once, also when the choice waits for a seat change still in progress) or Start tries the seat again, and while that seat's picture loads a short note there says so; a sound-source response that fails to load stops playback and shows a message there too, until it loads or another seat or sound source is chosen (the failure of a response the visitor has already moved on from, by choosing another seat or sound source, changes nothing: the newer choice plays as asked); a sound chosen in the sample menu or a file chosen in Upload while another plays replaces it; a microphone that cannot be started (none found, access refused, not available, or its sound cannot be connected), a sound that cannot be connected to the audio processing, and a Start that fails for another reason, is reported there too, without a dialog (the template's own dialogs remain for a browser without Web Audio and for an audio library that fails to load or to start); the press of Start itself asks the browser to let the sound start, also when the seat or its response is still loading or Start tries a seat whose picture failed (Safari and other WebKit browsers let sound start only from a press), and a Start that the browser has not let sound within about 3 seconds ends with a message there saying so, with Start ready to be pressed again, as the template does since 2026-10-29; and the offline helper is registered even when the page has finished loading first (M).
  • index.html (C, D, H): the menu label reads "Sound source" and a switch button between A and B is added (C); the 3-D library A-Frame is loaded without the async attribute, which failed on every fresh load in testing (D); a truth line visible at every screen width, a phone layout, Start Audio at the top of the panel, a note on the Mic mode, an Upload button and file-name text in English whatever the browser's language (the system's own file window keeps its language), no CAVERN branding, the arrow-key camera movement and phone-tilt control turned off, and the audio library loaded after the page (H).
  • scripts/audio.js (E, J, K, L): each of the four ambisonic channels is convolved separately (the stock single convolver treated the four channels as stereo and lost the direction) (E); the level is corrected for the device's sample rate, the dry (Ambisonic Mode off) path plays at a fixed level near the simulated sound, the sample is decoded once, a microphone whose sound cannot be connected is released at once, and a missing microphone is reported (J); a sample can carry its own fixed playback gain, applied once to the decoded sample so that Ambisonic Mode on and off stay comparable (K); Start begins at the chosen mode's level, so Ambisonic Mode plays no unconvolved sound before the response, only the newest response request is used (an older request's failure changes nothing), a response that fails to load stops playback instead of leaving the previous one playing, Stop ends the sound and lets the response's simulated reverberation ring out (as the template does since 2026-10-29), a change of seat or sound source and a failure fade out everything that sounds, a ringing reverberation included, in 25 ms, so no reverberation sounds under another seat or sound source or after a message that nothing is playing, Ambisonic Mode changes only the sound that is playing, never a reverberation still ringing, and a sound connects only once the browser lets the sound run: no call waits for the browser without a limit, audio that the browser has suspended or the device has interrupted (for example during a call) is asked to run, and a Start waits for it at most about 3 seconds instead of showing "Playing..." while nothing can sound; audio that the device interrupts while it is already playing (for example by a call) is not watched, as in the template, so "Playing..." can stay shown in silence while the interruption lasts, and after it if the browser leaves the sound suspended, until Stop and then Start are pressed (L).
  • sw.js (G, N): the offline helper fetches the tour's own files from the network first, so corrected pages reach returning visitors (G); it deletes only its own earlier caches, never those of other pages on the same site (N).
